The owner asked (2026-11-23) for *"a way to have the video playing like in the corner while you browse the site kind of like YouTube has, where you browse away from a video, but then it keeps playing the video in the corner, and you can go and open up and up again."*, more *"elegant and like automatic"* than the browser's picture-in-picture. **Acceptance criteria** (checks `scripts/verify-S198.sh` performs)

1. **It goes to the corner when you leave a playing video, as on YouTube.** While a clip plays in the player (the dialog from any of its three hosts, or the stage), any in-app route change (a link in the player such as its inning pill or game link, a header or footer link, the browser's Back or Forward) moves it to a corner player at the bottom right, playing on from the same time with no reload; so does a mini-player button in BT's tool row under the clip (YouTube's miniplayer button), which leaves the fan on the page beneath. A paused clip closes on leaving, as today. The same `<video>` element stays mounted, held in the player provider above the routes (AC 3) and moved, never re-created, so a browser that blocks a fresh `play()` without a gesture can't stop it. RTL from each of the three hosts: a playing clip, a route change, then the corner player holds the same `HTMLVideoElement` instance as before (identity check) and a `currentTime` no earlier than before; the mini-player button does the same without a route change; a paused clip leaves no corner player. A Playwright flow (`e2e/flows/`): play a clip on a game's Videos room, leave for the Box room, the clip still playing (its `paused` false, `currentTime` advancing), open the scoreboard, still playing.
2. **The corner player's controls, as on YouTube.** It shows the clip with play / pause, an expand control and a close control, and keeps the clip's title as its accessible name. Expand navigates to the video's own page (its link, B424: the page it was opened from, with that video's player open) and shows the clip in S196's modal, playing on from the same time; returning to that page by Back expands it the same way. Close stops the clip and removes the corner player. Opening another video while one plays in the corner replaces it: the new video opens in the full player and the corner player closes. Only one corner player exists at a time. RTL for each: expand (URL and the same element in the modal), Back to the video's page, close (`paused` true, no corner player), another video opened.
3. **One player, held above the routes.** The player's state lives in one place above the router's pages (React state lifted into a provider, as ADR-024 allows: `useState` and props, no proxy, observable or signal, and no second Zustand store), so a route change doesn't unmount it. `VideoPlayer` (`web/src/components/videos/VideoPlayer.tsx`) is rendered in exactly one place, the provider. A portal whose target changes remounts its children, so that is not the mechanism; instead the provider renders `VideoPlayer` once, through `createPortal`, into one container node it creates once and never replaces, and on each host change moves that node into the active host's slot with `appendChild` (the react-reverse-portal pattern), in a layout effect of the same commit that mounts the new host, before the old host unmounts, and never into a host whose mount is lazy or suspended, so moving between the modal, the stage and the corner player never creates a new `<video>` element (AC 1). S196's `PlayerDialog` tests render inside the provider and keep their assertions. Grep under `web/src/` outside tests: one site renders `<VideoPlayer`, in the provider; `PlayerDialog`, `PlayerStage` and the corner player render none.
4. **It stays out of the way.** At every width the corner player sits at the bottom right, about a third of the viewport's width at mobile and a fixed small width (about 400px) at medium and desktop, and covers neither the update notice (shell-scoreboard A6), the after-update pointer (A9), nor the site footer's Live strip (A13, A14): it sits above them (RTL or Playwright with each shown, checking their boxes don't overlap).

**Notes:**

- **Videos as links** (B424: a video's own URL opens the page it came from with the video open) and this story both touch how the open video is held; whichever lands second keeps the other's behavior and tests.
- **The provider's player is keyed by the clip alone (2026-11-25 focused backlog review).** At HEAD `PlayerStage` renders `` <VideoPlayer key={`${video.id}:${autoPlay}`} …> `` (`web/src/components/videos/PlayerStage.tsx`), so a change of `autoPlay` remounts the player with a new `<video>` element. Carried into the provider, that key breaks AC 1: the stage's first clip mounts with `autoPlay` false and the fan presses play in the element itself; leaving the page hands the clip to the corner player, which plays it, and if that host passes `autoPlay` true the key changes and the identity check fails with the clip back at its start. So the provider's one `VideoPlayer` is keyed by the clip (`video.id`) only, and `autoPlay` turning on reaches it through `VideoPlayer`'s existing effect for that (#423), never a remount (review-class checklist items 22 and 23).
