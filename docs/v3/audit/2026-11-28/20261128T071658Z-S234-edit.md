**Gap:** The strike-zone view draws every batter in the same spot in the same pose: `catcherViewBatter` (`packages/domain/src/catcher-view.ts`, drawn by `web/src/components/pitch/StrikeZoneChart.tsx`) places one image (`assets/batter.webp`), mirrored for left-handers and scaled to the batter's height, with its feet 38.5 in from the plate's centre and level with the middle of the plate. Real batters stand 25 to 43 in from the centre (median 36) and a median 11 in behind the plate's back tip, so BT's batter is about 20 in too far forward for a typical hitter (`docs/v3/SAVANT-CATALOGUE.md` § Batting stance, computed there; the agent's reading). Baseball Savant's batting stance page (`/visuals/batting-stance`) embeds a `vizData` array, one row per batter per side (95 fields): the hips' depth in the box and distance off the plate (inches), the feet's separation and angle (open or closed), six foot points (big toe, small toe and heel of each foot) at three moments (in feet; x from the plate's centre in the catcher's view, y from the back tip toward the pitcher, which the catalogue reads as BT's own plate frame), and where the bat meets the ball. Its default is qualified batters only (231 rows), which fails the owner's every-player test; with `minSwings=1` it answered 709 rows for 640 batters, every one with foot data, in about 2.5 MB of HTML. All of it is a season average, none per pitch (the same section). The owner wants the batter's full stance shown, not only where he stands: *"Second - the batting stance is something I would love to figure out either using 3d, prebaked assets, or 3d-to-2d projection mapping. Batter position is cool but actually showing the full position would be rad."* (owner, 2026-11-28).
