**Gap:** BT knows each pitch's type from MLB's live feed, but not how that pitch does for the pitcher over a season, or how a batter does against that type: MLB's API has pitch-type split codes (`pff`, `psl`) that return no rows from `statSplits`, and Baseball-Reference has none (`docs/v3/SAVANT-CATALOGUE.md` § Pitch-arsenal-stats). S104 catalogued Savant's player data and the owner set the tests (owner, 2026-11-28: *"I am only interested in data I can get 1) for every player, 2) which isn't available from other free sources, 3) which is a) reasonably useful to show users as is, or b) is reasonably useful to show in combination with another stat we can already easily get from mlb, or c) is usable to mathematically calculate something that is reasonably useful to show people."*); results by pitch type passed them, and the owner chose them: *"I LOVE the idea of pitch 1, especially the putaway pitch thing, the BAA for a given pitch, etc as long as it's not repetitive for every single batter. But as far as getting the data, yes."* Savant publishes them as a leaderboard file, but the owner was then asked (2026-11-28) *"S230 (results by pitch type: batting average, slugging, whiff rate per pitch type) downloads Savant's whole pitch-arsenal leaderboard file every day, which the legal report counts as the bulk-leaderboard risk. BT already stores every pitch with its type, so it could compute these itself in the same daily batch as the percentiles. Which should S230 do?"* and answered *"If we can compute it without exorbitant cost, do that"*, under the owner's cost rule (*"For the computation ones though, I want to focus on performance of the computation. I don't want to end up spending a ton on compute"*, 2026-11-28). BT already stores every pitch of every game it keeps, backfilled games included (S122 stores the full feed): its type (MLB's own `details.type` code, the same code a live pitch carries), the count after it, the call and the batted ball (`packages/domain/src/plays.ts`), and the at-bat's result. S233 extracts each final game's per-player facts once, in one statement a game, behind a gate for daily batches. This story adds the same facts per pitch type to that pass and totals them once a day in one statement.

**Impact:** Before S230, BT could say what a pitch was but not how well that pitch works for the pitcher, or how the batter does against it. S230 impact: BT works out for itself, from the pitches it already stores, each pitcher's results with each of his pitches and each batter's results against each pitch type, for every player, refreshed once a day at small cost, ready for S232 to show (a pitcher's put-away pitch, the average against his slider). No screen changes yet.

**Acceptance criteria** (checks `scripts/verify-S230.sh` performs)

1. **Per pitch type, per game, one function** (ADR-025). `packages/domain` exports `playerGamePitchTypeFacts(snapshot)`: per player, role (batter, pitcher) and pitch type (MLB's `details.type` code), counted through S233's predicates (`isPlateAppearance`, `isAtBat`, `isStrikeout`, `isWalk`, `isHit`, `totalBases`, `isSwing`, `isSwingingStrike`, `isBattedBall`, `isHardHit`):
    - **Per pitch:** pitches, swings, whiffs, batted balls, hard-hit batted balls, two-strike pitches, and put-aways: a two-strike pitch to which the at-bat's strikeout is credited by the rule below, so a strikeout on an automatic strike after a two-strike pitch is no put-away for that pitch (test).
    - **Per plate appearance:** plate appearances, at-bats, hits, total bases, strikeouts and walks, credited to the type of the at-bat's last pitch only when that pitch decided the result: it was put in play or hit the batter, or its after-count agrees with the event (three strikes for a strikeout, four balls for a walk). Otherwise the result is credited to `PITCH_TYPE_NONE` (`"none"`, a named sentinel, since the type is part of the key). That covers a strikeout on an automatic strike after the last pitch (fixture game 823823's at-bat 2: `CH`, `SL`, `CH`, `CU` to 2–2, then "Automatic Strike - Batter Timeout Violation") and a walk with no pitch (an automatic intentional walk). The rule follows Savant's convention of crediting a result to its last pitch; that convention is the agent's reading. A pitch with no type is counted under `PITCH_TYPE_NONE` too.
    - **Count before a pitch:** MLB's `count` on a pitch is the count after it (the mapper keeps only pitches, `functions/src/mappers/live.ts`), so the count before a pitch is the previous pitch's count (0–0 for the first). It is checked against the pitch's own call: a ball, and a hit-by-pitch (`H`), adds a ball (fixture game 823823's at-bat 20, pitch 5, goes from 1–2 to 2–2, so it was a real two-strike pitch); a called or swinging strike and a foul tip add a strike; a foul bunt (`L`) adds a strike at any count, and is the third strike at two strikes; any other foul adds a strike below two strikes; in play changes nothing. When the previous count and the call can't give the pitch's after-count (an automatic ball or strike in between), the before-count is unknown and the pitch is not counted as a two-strike pitch.
    - Tests:
        - in `functions/src/`: fixture game 823823, hand-counted for three named players, its at-bat 2 credited to `PITCH_TYPE_NONE`;
        - in `packages/domain`: hand-written snapshots for a walk with no pitch, an automatic ball mid at-bat (the next pitch's before-count unknown), a foul at two strikes, and a pitch with no type;
        - a consistency test on 823823: for every player and role, the sums over pitch types equal S233's `playerGameFacts` totals (pitches, swings, whiffs, plate appearances, strikeouts, walks, batted balls, hard-hit batted balls).
2. **Same pass, same statement.** The rows go in a sibling table, `player_game_pitch_type_facts` (season, game, player, role, pitch type, AC 1's counts), written in S233's per-game statement together with S233's facts and state record (all or none; the contract test runs on both adapters). This story raises `PLAYER_FACTS_EXTRACTOR_VERSION`, so S233's sweep re-extracts every kept game and adds their rows, and the gate holds the aggregate back until that pass ends (test: a game extracted before the raise gets its pitch-type rows on the new pass, and the aggregate waits for it). Extraction makes no extra statement and reads no extra document.
3. **Totalled once a day, in one statement.** When S233's `factsBatchDue` says so for a season, one set-based statement does the work. It sums the season's `player_game_pitch_type_facts` per player, role and pitch type into `player_pitch_type_results`, rates computed as `numeric`. It replaces the season's rows and writes its state row in `player_pitch_type_aggregations` (season, the facts watermark, aggregated at), and deletes pitch-type facts of seasons before the year before. The results are:
    - each count of AC 1;
    - usage % (a type's pitches ÷ the player's pitches in that role with a type, leaving out `PITCH_TYPE_NONE`, intentional balls `IN` and pitchouts `PO`, which are not pitch choices; BT's rule; those three rows' own usage % is null);
    - BA (hits ÷ at-bats), SLG (total bases ÷ at-bats), whiff % (whiffs ÷ swings), K % (strikeouts ÷ plate appearances), put-away % (put-aways ÷ two-strike pitches) and hard-hit % (hard-hit ÷ batted balls), each null when its denominator is 0.

    A season is totalled only when its facts changed, so the year before runs about once after it ends. **Run by the cron:** a cron step in its own scope (`inDatabaseScope`, kind `cron`, job `pitch-type-results`), called from `scheduled` in `functions/src/worker.ts`, asks `factsBatchDue` each run with this story's state row and runs the aggregate when it answers due: an idle run is the gate's 1 statement, a due run 2. Tests on a fake clock, through the real gate: idle; facts changed and a day passed, one aggregate; facts changed within the day, none; a game claimed inside its lease, delayed until the lease ends. Tests on PGlite through the real statement: generated facts with hand-worked results, a zero denominator, and the retention delete.
4. **Cost, counted, against the estimate.** The estimate, written here so "without exorbitant cost" can be checked:
    - Fixture game 823823 gives 156 pitch-type rows (22 batters, 10 pitchers, 339 pitches). At about 160 rows a game that is about 390,000 rows a season and about 780,000 for the two seasons kept, about 100 MB with the key and indexes.
    - The daily statement reads one season's rows once and writes about 8,000.
