**Gap:** S233 keeps each player's per-game facts in a small table, and nothing ranks them. The owner chose that BT rank players itself rather than copy Savant's percentiles: asked (2026-11-28) *"The legal report says to compute percentile rankings ourselves (a sort over facts BT stores), but you chose S233 as 'fetched, not computed' from Savant. Which should stand?"*, the owner answered *"Compute our own (Recommended)"*, the option reading *"Rewrite S233 so BT ranks qualified players itself from stored facts and labels the values as BT's. Numbers can differ slightly from Savant's."* That supersedes the owner's earlier *"I like your recommendations."* (S104's: fetch them), which answered the owner's first words on them: *"percentile rankings for qualified players is worth getting, but if we can compute it from the other stats we already get that's another story. I don't know if we really know that though; the percentile stuff usually is not a normal stat and I'm not sure they even show which value is the one used"* (owner, 2026-11-28). On cost: *"For the computation ones though, I want to focus on performance of the computation. I don't want to end up spending a ton on compute"* (owner, 2026-11-28). How Savant ranks, which BT follows where it was measured: `round(100 × players strictly below ÷ (pool − 1))`, 0 shown as 1, ties shared, a pool that differs by metric (246 qualified batters at 2.1 PA per team game for most metrics, 1.25 for pitchers; every batter with a batted ball for max exit velocity; 209 bat-tracking qualifiers for bat speed), and every pitcher metric unverified (`docs/v3/SAVANT-CATALOGUE.md` § Percentile rankings).

**Impact:** Before S237, BT could not say how a player ranks against the league. S237 impact: BT ranks qualified players itself each day, from the facts it keeps (exit velocity, hard-hit rate, barrel rate, strikeout and walk rates, whiffs and chases, bat speed; pitch speed, spin and extension for pitchers), labels the rankings as BT's own, and serves them for a view the owner designs later, at the cost of one small database statement a day. No screen changes yet.

1a. **Barrel rate, by MLB's rule.** Asked (2026-11-28) *"S237 ranks only measured facts BT stores. Should BT's percentiles include barrel rate, computed from exit velocity and launch angle by MLB's published barrel rule?"*, the owner chose *"Yes, by MLB's rule (Recommended)"*.
    - **The rule:** MLB's glossary (https://www.mlb.com/glossary/statcast/barrel, read 2026-11-28) says: *"To be Barreled, a batted ball requires an exit velocity of at least 98 mph. At that speed, balls struck with a launch angle between 26-30 degrees always garner Barreled classification. For every mph over 98, the range of launch angles expands. For example: A ball traveling 99 mph always earns 'Barreled' status when struck between 25-31 degrees. Add one more mph -- to reach 100 -- and the range grows another three degrees, to 24-33. Every additional mph over 100 increases the range another two to three degrees until an exit velocity of 116 mph is reached. At that threshold, the Barreled designation is assigned to any ball with a launch angle between eight and 50 degrees."*
    - **Where the glossary is silent:** it gives exact bounds only at 98, 99, 100 and 116 mph. Between those speeds BT moves both bounds linearly: from 98 to 99 to 100 mph (26–30°, 25–31°, 24–33°), and from 100 to 116 mph the lower bound from 24° to 8° and the upper from 33° to 50° (about 2.1° of range per mph, inside the glossary's "two to three"). It keeps 8–50° above 116 mph. That interpolation is BT's reading, said so in the function's comment and in SAVANT-DATA.
    - **One definition:** one predicate in `packages/domain`, `isBarrelByMlbRule(launchSpeed, launchAngle)`, beside S233's `isHardHit`. It is not S103's stored `isBarrel`, which is Savant's own flag.
    - **Counted in S233's pass:** S233's `playerGameFacts` gains a barrel count for each batter (batted balls the predicate accepts). That needs a new `player_game_facts` column (a migration), the ports type, the in-memory double, the Postgres adapter and the contract test. This story makes that one addition to S233's facts and raises `PLAYER_FACTS_EXTRACTOR_VERSION`, so S233's sweep re-extracts the kept games before the gate lets a ranking run.
    - **Tests:**
        - the boundaries: 97.9 mph at 28° (no); 98 mph at 25.9°, 26°, 30° and 30.1° (no, yes, yes, no); 98.5 mph at 25.4°, 25.5°, 30.5° and 30.6° (no, yes, yes, no); 99 mph at 24.9°, 25°, 31° and 31.1° (no, yes, yes, no); 100 mph at 24° and 33° (yes); 108 mph, inside the interpolated range, at 15.9°, 16°, 41.5° and 41.6° (no, yes, yes, no); 116 mph at 8° and 50° (yes); 120 mph at 7.9° and 50.1° (no, no);
        - a missing launch angle (no barrel);

2. **One statement, generated from the table.** One function in `functions/src/` builds the ranking statement from `PERCENTILE_METRICS`, so no metric's rule is written twice. In one statement it sums `player_game_facts` per player for the season, applies each metric's pool and minimum, ranks with `percent_rank()` computed as `numeric`, inverted where lower is better, writes `round(100 × percent_rank)` with 0 shown as 1, replaces the season's rows in `player_percentiles` (season, player, metric, BT's value, percentile), and writes the ranking's state row in `player_percentile_rankings` (season, the facts watermark it ranked, ranked at). A pool of no players ranks nothing, and a pool of one player gives that player no percentile (no row). No TypeScript copy of the formula exists. Tests on PGlite through the real statement, on generated facts: ties share a percentile; a value landing at exactly .5 rounds as Postgres `numeric` rounds it (half away from zero), tested; pools of 0, 1 and 2; an inverted metric; a player under a minimum count; a traded player against the league's team games.
