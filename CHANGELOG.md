# Changelog

What shipped and why it matters — written for anyone, not just engineers. Each entry
leads with the **impact**: what was true before, what's different now. A **Details** line
underneath gives the technical trail (story ID, ADR, commit) for anyone who wants to go
deeper. Related stories that are clearly part of one larger effort are nested under a
bold section header; a change that stands on its own gets its own bullet.

This project is pre-launch — there's no public release yet, so entries are grouped by
theme rather than version number. **Most of what's below is backend work you can't see
yet** in the running app; it's the pipe that later screens will read from. Entries say so
explicitly where that's the case. For what's still ahead, see
[`docs/v3/BACKLOG.md`](docs/v3/BACKLOG.md).

## Download is back on the video player

- **Before, saving a video meant opening it with Direct link and saving it from the browser's own player. Now the player's Download button saves any MLB clip, a condensed game included, straight to your device, passed along as it arrives so even a long file never has to fit in a phone's memory.** It appears once your browser is running this version of the app. Details: **S151** — the service worker answers a same-site `/download/clip?url=…&name=…` route by streaming MLB's MP4 through as an attachment, only for MP4s on MLB's clip hosts (`isMlbClipMp4`), and the palette shows Download only while the worker in control answers its ping; checked by the owner in Safari, Chrome and Firefox.

## A progress bar for backlog runs

- **Before, the owner had to read back through a backlog run's updates to tell how far it had got. Now every run prints a progress bar, the share of its planned stories and bugs finished with what's done, in progress, parked and next, after each merge or park and at the top of the run's summary; it reads the same in the terminal and the Claude app and stands out while scrolling.** Details: **S221** — `runs.ts` `runProgress` (each item's state from its newest item event; a `held` item leaves the total) and `runs.ts progress --journal <path>` (a plain-text 20-cell `█`/`░` bar), the run-level `plan` event (`claims.ts run event plan --items <ID>,…`), and run-backlog's SKILL.md saying when to log the plan and print the bar.

## Anonymous counts of the videos fans watch

- **Before, Baseball Theater had no idea which videos fans actually play or finish. Now, when you play a clip from a game's Videos tab, BT adds one to an anonymous daily count of how often that clip is started and how often it is watched to the end, with nothing about who watched, so a later feature can learn which clips to show first. The Privacy policy says so, and nothing on screen changes.** Details: **S174** — `POST /api/v1/clips/:mediaId/views` (204) into the `clip_engagement` table (per clip, game, UTC day, event and place in the ranked list; one atomic `count + 1`), sent once per opening by the Videos room's dialog through `navigator.sendBeacon` (`sendClipView`); a clip counts as finished at 90% played or at its end (`clipCompleted`); `pnpm clips:engagement-export --from --to` writes the counts as JSON lines for the ranking model (S185).

## A drawing of every ballpark, ready for a spray chart

## Baseball Savant's extras on every pitch

- **Before, Baseball Theater knew how each pitch moved but not what Baseball Savant makes of it. Now each pitch carries Savant's extras — how likely a batted ball was to be a hit, whether it was barreled (struck with the ideal mix of speed and angle), where it landed, whether a home run would have left other ballparks, the swing's speed, sword swings (a swing so fooled that the batter barely gets the bat through), Savant's own strike-zone call, how many times the pitcher had been through the order and the exact pitch of each ABS challenge (a player asking the automated ball-strike system to review the umpire's call) — filling in as Savant publishes them, without ever holding up the live game, and noting when each figure arrived so the delay can be measured once games resume.** Details: **S103** — `GameSnapshot.savant` from `baseballsavant.mlb.com/gf` through `MlbStatsClient.fetchSavantGameFeed`, pulled by the game's ingest object after each poll (`savantPullDue`, `pullSavant`) and merged by version (`mergeSavantPitches`); MLB's Terms of Use and the owner's go-ahead are recorded in `docs/v3/SAVANT-DATA.md`; recorded as `fixtures/raw/savant-gf-823823.json`.

## Real MLB game data, end to end

Before this work, the app only knew how to replay a handful of hand-picked practice games
saved to disk. This effort teaches it to talk to MLB's actual live game service — the
foundation every future screen (live scores, box scores, standings) will read from.

- **The backend can now fetch a real MLB game — in progress or finished — and understand
  every part of it**: score, inning-by-inning detail, every pitch (speed, location, spin),
  highlight videos, and full team/player info. Previously it only understood a few
  practice games that had been manually shaped to fit.
  Details: S11 (typed contracts for MLB's data), S12 (the live connection + translators),
  S13 (standings, article content, player bios), S23 (near-total field coverage with an
  automatic "did we miss something" check). _(2026-09-08)_

- **Live games now update automatically while they're being played, and the app stops
  checking once they're over** — so MLB's servers only get contacted for games that are
  actually happening right now, not once per person looking at the page.
  Details: S21 (raw MLB data gets turned into the app's own stable, queryable records),
  S16 (an active-window polling loop, per ADR-002). _(2026-09-09)_

- **Looking up an old, finished game no longer serves data that's stuck from whenever it
  was first loaded — but it also won't hammer MLB's servers just because a page got
  opened twice.** A background safety valve refreshes it once, then waits before trying
  again.
  Details: S17 — refresh-on-read with request de-duplication and a cooldown. _(2026-09-09)_

- **A finished game's entire play-by-play can now be replayed at any point or any speed,
  without ever asking MLB for it again.** The full sequence of what happened is captured
  once, right after the final out, and stored for good.
  Details: S22 — captures MLB's official timecode-by-timecode diff log for a finished

  Details: S15 (`pnpm mlb:scan-drift`), S14 (a recorder that keeps the app's practice-game
  test data up to date from the real MLB feed on demand). _(2026-09-09)_
